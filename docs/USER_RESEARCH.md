# User research plan

V1 was built from my own use as a backcountry skier in Boise. That's one data point. The next step is
to check the assumptions with 5–8 people before building V1.1.

## Who to talk to
- 3–4 backcountry skiers (ski partners, local Facebook/Reddit groups, avalanche course classmates)
- 1–2 resort skiers who drive 2+ hours
- 1–2 "snow-curious" non-skiers (hunters, snowmobilers, water-supply-curious locals)

## Assumptions to test
| # | Assumption | Signal it's true | Signal it's false |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | People choose destinations by drive time first | They describe a time budget ("I'll drive 3 hours max") | They have 1–2 fixed spots and never consider others |
| A2 | SNOTEL depth is trusted as a proxy for conditions | They already check SNOTEL or ask "how much snow at X" | They only trust trip reports or avalanche forecasts |
| A3 | "% of normal" is meaningful to non-hydrologists | They can explain it back | Confusion or indifference |
| A4 | Mobile is the main device | They plan on phones | Desk/laptop planning on Thursday night |

## Interview guide (20 min)
1. Walk me through how you decided where to ski last weekend. *(Don't mention the app yet.)*
2. What sources did you check? In what order? What was annoying?
3. How far are you willing to drive for a day trip? What changes that?
4. *(Show the app, ?from=<their city>)* What do you think this is telling you? What would you do next?
5. What's missing that would make you open this instead of what you use now?

## Synthesis
Capture quotes into a simple table (assumption → evidence → confidence), then update
PRODUCT.md's roadmap. Record what changed, and why, in DECISIONS.md.
